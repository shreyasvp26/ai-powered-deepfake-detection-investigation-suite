\begin{abstract}
Most deepfake detection work focuses on a binary question: is a face real or fake? But forensic investigators need more than that - they want to know \textit{how} the manipulation was done. We introduce the \textbf{Dual-Stream Attribution Network (DSAN)}, designed specifically for this attribution task. DSAN feeds each face crop through two streams in parallel: an RGB stream (EfficientNet-B4) that picks up spatial manipulation artifacts, and a frequency stream (ResNet-18) that processes Spatial Rich Model noise residuals alongside 2D FFT spectral features. These are merged through a \textit{gated bilinear fusion} layer. A key finding is that na\"ive multi-head attention over just two tokens collapses to scalar interpolation, effectively killing the frequency stream; our per-dimension gating sidesteps this problem. The training objective combines cross-entropy, supervised contrastive learning, and a blending-mask decoder inspired by Face~X-ray. We embed DSAN in a modular forensic pipeline covering face extraction, binary detection, temporal consistency analysis, and \textit{dual} Grad-CAM++ explainability across both spatial and spectral domains. After training on a 70/30 mix of c23 and c40 FaceForensics++ frames with identity-safe splits, the system reaches \textbf{0.941 AUC and 91.2\% accuracy} on binary detection and \textbf{90.0\% macro F1} on four-class attribution on the standard c23 test split, with an Expected Calibration Error of 0.043. Cross-dataset probes on Celeb-DF~v2 and DFDC preview show the expected $\sim$22-point AUC drop, which we report honestly rather than tuning around.
\end{abstract}
